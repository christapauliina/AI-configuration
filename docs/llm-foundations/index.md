# AI Fundamentals

Broadly, we tend to think that you simply gather training data, feed it through a neural network, and voilà you have an LLM. There is an underlying assumption that a bigger dataset and a larger neural network automatically result in a more capable and intelligent model. The reality is more complex. In this section, we take a slightly more in-depth look under the hood of these published models.

At its simplest, creating a publish-ready AI model includes three main stages: creating a base model, teaching the base model how to behave to get an aligned model, and optionally using advanced technologies to enhance the model's capabilities.

In most use cases, we don't actually build AI models ourselves; instead, we use already published models and configure them to best fulfill our needs. While these foundations aren't strictly mandatory to understand for configuring your LLM models or agents, understanding these core concepts will help you grasp why and how specific techniques work in practice.

### Goals

Most online resources about AI or LLMs tend to fall into one of two extremes: high-level, superficial summaries that gloss over the technical reality, or dense academic papers packed with intimidating mathematical equations. The main goal of the AI Fundamentals page is to land right in the middle.

This section provides a comprehensive yet accessible look under the hood of modern LLMs, focusing on the underlying phenomena and engineering decisions that explain why models work the way they do. No prior knowledge of LLMs is required. It is designed to work as a guided introduction that builds the intuition and vocabulary you need, and it can also serve as a stepping stone if you later want to dive deeper into the technical and mathematical side of LLMs.

To keep things approachable, some technical and mathematical details are simplified. Whenever this happens, the simplification is explicitly pointed out, so you always know where the full picture is more nuanced.
